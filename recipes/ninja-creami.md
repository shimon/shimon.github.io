### Lemon-Basil Cream {#lemon-basil}

**Subtle, sophisticated, and not too sweet. Excellent yogurt flavor and texture. Amounts confirmed.**

- ½ cup unsweetened plain coconut yogurt
- ½ cup canned full-fat coconut milk
- 3 tbsp instant lemon pudding powder
- 6 basil leaves
- 2 tbsp agave

Blend; follow the [shared method](#method). **Next time:** repeat unchanged. This is the best-documented winner.

### Spicy Pineapple {#spicy-pineapple}

**Surprisingly good. A fun palate cleanser between creamier flavors.**

- Plain crushed pineapple, with juice
- Selected pineapple/jalapeño pieces from the bettergoods jalapeño-pineapple jar, to taste
- Agave only if needed

Blend plain pineapple first. Add small portions from the spicy jar, tasting between additions; stop at pleasant heat. Follow the [shared method](#method).

**Do not use the whole spicy jar by default:** it can be painfully hot. Successful trial quantities were not recorded; the method above is a proposed way to control heat, not a measured reconstruction. Record the plain/spicy amounts next time.

## Shared method {#method}

1. Blend to a smooth base (whisk powder-only mixes). Thaw frozen fruit enough to blend; don't spin loose frozen chunks.
2. Fill only to the container's **MAX FILL** line. Reserve excess; don't dilute to reach the line. Fit the storage lid and close the Swirl nozzle per its manual.
3. Freeze upright and level for **at least 24 hours**.
4. Process and serve. **Round 1:** most batches used **Sorbet**; Piña Colada and Butterscotch-Banana also worked with soft-serve dispensing. Exact soft-serve subprograms weren't recorded. Follow the model's dedicated Soft Serve instructions for dispensing.
5. If crumbly after the first spin, try **RE-SPIN before adding liquid**. Follow the manual for fully refrozen leftovers. Dispense close to serving time.

**Ingredients:** cream of coconut is sweetened, unlike unsweetened coconut cream. Check actual pudding/mix labels for dairy and gluten. Recipes with unspecified can sizes may exceed one container.

## Trial notes & misses {#trial-notes}

### Round 1 summary

Most bases were blended, then processed on Sorbet. Piña Colada and Butterscotch-Banana were also dispensed as soft serve. Exact processing sequences and re-spin counts weren't recorded.

| Recipe | Household verdict | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Piña Colada | Easy; everyone loved it | Keep; clarify can size |
| Butterscotch-Banana | Tasty; Clara particularly enjoyed it | Keep; clarify quantities |
| Lemon-Basil | Very good; subtle; yogurt gives great texture; not too sweet | Repeat unchanged |
| Jalapeño-Pineapple | Surprisingly good; enjoyable heat when controlled | Keep; measure spicy portion |
| Spicy mixed canned fruit | Okay, but worse than pineapple | Prefer pineapple |
| Watermelon-Strawberry | Okay, not great; watermelon itself was mediocre | Better fruit before changing the formula |
| Beet-Cherry-Grapefruit | Awful; overwhelmingly beet-heavy; great color | Retire original formula |

### Watermelon-Strawberry — middling result

**Original formula:** 2 cups watermelon cubes + 1 cup strawberries + 3 tbsp corn syrup + squeeze of lemon.

The fruit-heavy approach is worth keeping, but this batch's watermelon was mediocre. Try only with good-tasting fruit; no need to repeat it in the next round.

### Beet-Cherry-Grapefruit — failed formula

**Historical amounts, not a recommendation:** ½ can sliced beets + ¼ cup maraschino cherries + ½ can red grapefruit + 2 tbsp corn syrup. Can sizes and retained liquid weren't recorded.

Far too much beet, even for a beet lover. Beautiful color did not redeem the flavor. If revisited, use **one-fifth or less of the original beet amount**, adding gradually to a fruit-first base. That reduction is a proposed rescue, not a tested recipe. Skip for now.

### Spicy pineapple — correction to the original draft

The draft called for an entire jalapeño-pineapple jar, ½ can crushed pineapple, and 2 tbsp agave. **The actual successful approach used the spicy jar selectively**, and all heat came from that jar. Do not treat the full-jar draft as the tested recipe. Substituting other canned fruit when pineapple ran out was acceptable but less successful.
