Shallow pan-fried · Gluten-free & dairy-free · 1 lb chicken

Equipment: Wide heavy skillet, tongs, shallow dredging dish, mixing bowl, instant-read thermometer, wire rack over a tray.

Read left to right. Rows are ingredient groups; combine groups only when the grid says to. The “During the rest” column shows preparation you can do while the chicken rests.
Ingredient groupPrepareDuring the restCombine & cook
A | Chicken + seasoning
1 lb chicken tenders
1 egg white
½ tsp fine salt
½ tsp garlic powder
¼ tsp white or black pepper		 Pat chicken dry; gently flatten thick pieces to about ½ inch.

Toss with egg white and seasonings.		 Rest 10 minutes.

Meanwhile, prepare B and the rack; then heat C. Stay near the hot oil.

A + B: Dredge
Coat only the next batch; shake off excess for a thin dusting.

Into C: Fry
Lay away from you; do not crowd. Cook about 2–3 minutes per side, turning once the crust is set. Check the thickest part for 165°F.

Drain
Transfer to the rack.
B | Dry coating
¼ cup cornstarch
¼ cup white rice flour
Optional: ¼ tsp smoked paprika		 Mix in a shallow dish. Keep separate from A until ready to fry.
C | Frying oil
Neutral high-heat oil (canola, peanut, or refined avocado)		 Add about ¼ inch to the skillet. Heat over medium-high to about 350°F; adjust as needed.
D | Finish
A little salt; lemon wedges		 Set aside for serving. Set a wire rack over a tray. Salt lightly while hot. Add lemon just before eating.

Between batches: Skim browned crumbs and let the oil return to temperature. Dredge the next batch during this wait. Use clean tongs for cooked chicken; discard coating that touched raw chicken.

Format & exceptions: Adapted Tabular Recipe Notation, devised by Michael Chu of Cooking for Engineers (format explanation), with an explicit parallel-work column. Repeated batches and temperature-dependent cooking do not fit a single forward flow: repeat the dredge–fry–drain cycle, and use 165°F rather than the clock. Hot-oil frying needs attention; it is not a hands-off waiting period.

Ingredient note: Use white rice flour, not sweet/glutinous rice flour. Choose gluten-free-labeled ingredients if needed.

Suggested recipe; not yet kitchen-tested.
